*What a turn is made of — a free mandatory phase where you answer your own people, then one action that ends the turn — and the growing list of things now competing for that single slot.*

## What it is

A turn is one nation's opportunity to respond to its people and then act in the world. The current design separates mandatory internal politics from the player's ordinary action so that answering a movement does not consume the same slot as doing what the answer requires.

## How it works in the game

At the start of a nation's turn, any active [[Movement demands|movement demands]] must be answered before play can continue. Reading and answering those demand cards is **free and mandatory** ([[Rulings - Politics#Politics ruling 12]]).

After that phase, the nation normally has **one action**. A proposal such as asking another nation to form a [[Unions|union]] uses that action under the current design precedent ([[Rulings - Politics#Politics ruling 19]]). What the government promised in response to a movement can therefore be free to say while the act that fulfils the promise can still consume the ordinary turn action.

A [[Federation]] complicates the structure by having a short turn of its own under an elected federation leader in addition to the member nations' turns ([[Rulings - Politics#Politics ruling 25]]). A member whose petition to leave the federation is refused may declare departure anyway, which also needs a place in the turn structure ([[Rulings - Politics#Politics ruling 29]]).

Martial law is designed as another government response, but whether declaring it consumes the ordinary action has been explicitly deferred because the designer has already said the one-action structure needs a wider redesign ([[Rulings - Politics#Politics ruling 14]]).

The movement-demand phase, union proposals and federation turns are not currently implemented as described.

## Questions for the designer

- **The one-action rule is already marked for redesign.** Ruling 19 depends on that rule rather than standing independently, and ruling 14 explicitly defers martial-law timing to the same future mechanics pass. Any action-cost wording here should be revisited when round 7 changes the turn structure.
- **Federation turn order:** ruling 25 establishes a federation turn but the exact placement relative to member turns and the election interval remain to be specified.
