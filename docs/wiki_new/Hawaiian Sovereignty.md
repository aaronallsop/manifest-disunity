# Hawaiian Sovereignty

> [!abstract] At a glance

## What it is

Hawaiian Sovereignty is a expand movement rooted in Hawaii. It wants sovereignty; land restoration, and the intended political position carrying that demand is [[Distributism]].

As a **Expand** movement, this movement pressures its own government to take additional ground rather than moving the movement's own ground ([[Rulings - Secession#Secession ruling 39]]).

Its adjective is **indigenous**. In the movement design, the adjective describes what kind of concession can remove the want rather than what territorial result the movement is pursuing ([[Rulings - Secession#Secession ruling 43]]; [[Rulings - Politics#Politics ruling 11a]]).

Its homeland is 5 counties (3 core), which merge into 5 Areas (3 core). The movement's current growth ceiling is **0.45**; an Area leaves at 0.40. Reachable.. The general Area departure line is 0.40 movement share ([[Rulings - Politics#Politics ruling 10]]).

At setup it **spawns on a roll** and its opening condition is **growing**. Opening movement presence and size are resolved before the player chooses a nation, while movements that are born later can arise during play ([[Rulings - Secession#Secession ruling 14]]; [[Rulings - Secession#Secession ruling 15]]; [[Rulings - Secession#Secession ruling 42]]).

The intended political position is [[Distributism]]. The running game still assigns this movement the older **Distributist — the game still carries the old six ideologies** label, because the ten-position [[The political board|political board]] has not yet replaced the six-ideology implementation ([[Rulings - Politics#Politics ruling 2]]; [[Rulings - Politics#Politics ruling 41]]).

An Expand movement points outward: its own people pressure their government to acquire additional ground. The movement can exist at setup when the condition already exists, even though Expand was originally treated as one of the verbs normally born during play ([[Rulings - Secession#Secession ruling 42]]; [[Rulings - Secession#Secession ruling 44]]).

As with other movements, its members contribute to the movement's political pressure rather than simply disappearing into the government's support base; if internal conflict becomes military, movement members are intended to join the movement militia instead of the government army ([[Rulings - Politics#Politics ruling 6]]; [[Rulings - Politics#Politics ruling 7]]).
