*The four fights over who inherits a country that came apart — a Free Texas, the California Republic, the Confederacy and the Thirteen Colonies — who may claim each, how a reunification movement declares a winner instead of founding another new country, the thirty-three pairs that open permanently hostile because of it, and why Austin is the legitimate Texas and the other four are rebels.*

## What it is

A reunification contest is a struggle between several successor governments that all claim to inherit the same broken country. A reunification movement does not create another new state; it eventually chooses which existing claimant wins that inheritance.

## How it works in the game

Reunification movements operate at several scales and are explicitly contests among claimants ([[Rulings - Secession#Secession ruling 19]]). When such a movement succeeds, it **declares a winner rather than founding another country** ([[Rulings - Secession#Secession ruling 18]]).

The design has four major contests: **Texas**, **California**, the **Confederacy**, and the old **Thirteen Colonies / United States succession**. The capital contest is specifically Washington D.C., Philadelphia and New York City; Boston is not a claimant ([[Rulings - Secession#Secession ruling 20]]).

California has five claimant governments — Los Angeles, Bay Area, Riverside, Southern California and Northern California. Cascadia is deliberately outside that contest ([[Rulings - Military#Military ruling 32]]).

Texas has five claimant governments, but they are not legally equal in the story. **Austin is the legitimate continuation of Texas**; Dallas, Houston, San Antonio and El Paso are breakaway governments ([[Rulings - Military#Military ruling 19]]).

Contest claimants begin with a permanent **Hostile floor**. Ordinary hostility can cool in the rest of the game, but these rivalries do not cool below Hostile while the inheritance contest remains active ([[Rulings - Military#Military ruling 17]]). The completed claimant lists produce **33 permanent opening Hostile pairs**.

The floor freezes only the minimum state. Claimants can still escalate to [[War]], enter a cease-fire, and return to Hostile afterward; the relationship is not frozen in one condition forever.

Their economic relationships follow normal Hostility rules. Existing trade or transit agreements can run to the end of their terms, but new ones cannot be signed while the pair remains Hostile ([[Rulings - Military#Military ruling 20]]).

Political behaviour can eventually end a contest without conquest. A movement that is repeatedly strung along can change what it wants; if people stop wanting their old country restored, they have stopped contesting that inheritance ([[Rulings - Military#Military ruling 30]]).

Reunification claimants are also blocked from joining a full [[Unions|union]] while they carry the contest modifier ([[Rulings - Politics#Politics ruling 31]]). A federation may provide a different political answer without erasing the contest movement itself.

The contest system is not implemented; in the running game a reunification-typed movement can still behave like ordinary secession and create yet another state.

## The story behind it

Texas is the richest example. Austin is surrounded by four rival claimants, all permanently Hostile, yet its legitimacy gives it a political asset the others do not have. Because wars end by agreement, Austin's signature can matter even when it is economically or militarily weaker. California creates a different version with five claimants and a sixth California-derived political project — Cascadia — deliberately excluded from the inheritance fight.

## Questions for the designer

- **Full claimant table:** the page should eventually carry one canonical table for all four contests, because earlier source tables retain superseded claimant lists even when later rulings correct them.
- **Contest-ending condition:** the sources imply a contest can end when the relevant movement changes its objective, but the exact formal event that removes the permanent Hostile floor should be stated explicitly in implementation.
- **Union/federation breadth:** ruling 31 clearly blocks contest claimants from a union; how broadly that restriction applies to federation membership remains open.
