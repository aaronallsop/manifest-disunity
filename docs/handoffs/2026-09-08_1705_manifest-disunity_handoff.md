## 3. The rule you must not get wrong

**A design session does not touch code or data.** Not `js/`, `tests/`, `css/`, `build/`,
`index.html`, `dev.html`, `server.py`; not `data/` or `content/`; not `DESIGN.md`; not `docs/spec/`
without permission. You write in `docs/design/`, and may append to `DECISIONS.md`, `docs/deferred.md`
and `docs/FUTURE-IDEAS.md`. **You MAY read code** — this session read it four times and it changed
four answers. If you find a bug, write it down and move on.

**And one this project learned the hard way, which bit again tonight in a small way:** anything with
quotes and line breaks — the Control Board especially — is edited **directly with the file editor**,
never generated by a shell script with three layers of quoting. A heredoc failed on the first attempt
this session for exactly that reason.

## 4. What round 2 settled

**Ruling 1 is the frame and everything is measured against it.** Aaron: *"This isn't a war game… I
want a game where war impacts your economy and your diplomacy and your internal relations, and they
all interact."* Conquest is not the system the others serve; it is a system that happens **to** them.

**Ruling 2 is the spine.** Two nations are always in one named state:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Peace** | You can do anything with the nation |
| **Peace-treaty** | Signed, with stipulations. Breaking it has a huge impact |
| **Hostile** | Close to war without war |
| **Cease-fire** | All the impacts of war, but you cannot attack |
| **War** | Trade prohibited, and you may attack |
| **Subject / Allied** | Deferred by Aaron — **but ruling 4's fifth cause needs Allied, so take it inside this round** |

**The rest, in one line each.** The state is **shared, not directed** (5) — the one-sided version is
banked as **F18**. Refusing peace is allowed and free (6). Every state has an exit and three of the
four are clocks; only two transitions need both to agree (7). A cease-fire is a fixed term (3) ending
in a negotiation whose default is **Hostile, not War** (8). Five causes of hostility, **none of them a
military act** (4), and four costs (9). Ground changes hands at the settlement (10); war stays simple
where the player touches it (11); a fight is Field against Border shown as a percentage (12). Held
ground carries **`occupied-war`**, **`occupied`** or **`occupied-movement`** (13) — so **a peace treaty
is a title deed**. An attack has two outcomes and the price was paid before the dice (14). A treaty
has four levers (15). And **a movement makes demands answered by implement / decline / tell them to
wait** (16).

**What ruling 16 closed, and it is worth knowing.** Round 1's **finding D** — five of the six movement
verbs had no government response, because the four release valves only ever answered *Separate* — was
handed to politics as unsolved. Ruling 16 is the general form. **It is also the "what do I do on a
Tuesday" answer** the ideation plan demands of every round, and round 1's fifth scenario stalled on
exactly it.

## 5. Four things verified in the code this session, each of which changed an answer

Recorded because a design session that reads the build gives better answers than one that guesses.

1. **There is no war.** Unless a civil war fires **inside the attacker**, ground changes hands with
   no roll at all — the defender's Border allocation, readiness and armed population are read only as
   a multiplier on the attacker's own civil-war score. Taking land is a purchase.
2. **The fight Aaron described is already built.** Field against Border as a share, coalition armies
   at a discount, the distance penalty folded in — **and already displayed**, captioned *"How the
   fight goes — an army at the end of its supply line"*, as a multiplier rather than as odds.
3. **The attack price is debited BEFORE the roll.** This killed my proposal that a failed attack
   should cost readiness: the money is gone either way, so the odds price themselves.
4. **Crises trigger over the stocks and elections read war weariness.** Which is what makes ruling 6
   safe — a government that will not make peace is removed by its own electorate.
