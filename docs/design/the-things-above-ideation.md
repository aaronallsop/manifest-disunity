## Questions inherited from the story of the break-up (6 September)

1. **When did it happen?** *Answered 6 September (secession ruling 11): Texas declared in spring
   2034, two years before the opening; the game starts after the whole chain has run.* What remains
   for this round: two years is eight turns of memory decay, and every back-dated grudge in the
   story is stamped inside that window — the round should decide whether the opening memories are
   dated to their real moments across the two years or all to "two years ago". *Built: memories
   decay by turn; the honeymoon is four turns; the game opens 1 March 2036.*

2. **What "the United States" is now, and what Reunification means.** A small armed federal remnant
   exists — Washington, its martial-law ring, the rest of Virginia and the Baltimore region — and
   recognises nobody. Is winning by reunification *joining it*, *replacing it*, or *rebuilding the
   country around yourself against it*? Does playing as Washington get a different victory from
   playing as Dallas? *Built: Reunification = the seats of government, half the people, half the
   economy, and floors under two stocks.*

3. **Twenty-year agreements on a quarterly clock.** The New England–Rochester deal is eighty turns;
   the longest term the game allows is twenty — five years. Are longer terms allowed, and what does
   a five-year cap do to a story that thinks in generations? *Built: durations of 2 / 4 / 8 / 20
   turns, ruled in D163.*

4. **What the opening newspaper says.** The story is a page; the edition is three lines. *(Shared
   with events.)*

5. **What a nation knows about a tier-3 zone, or a vassal, or a bloc it is not in.** Information —

6. **The single action, against a story full of standing arrangements.** Vassalage, alliances, blocs
