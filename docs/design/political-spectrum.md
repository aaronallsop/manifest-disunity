# The political spectrum — three axes, twenty-seven positions

**Written 9 September 2026.** The reference table for the politics rebuild. Every position that
exists in the model, what it is, and who has actually held it in the real world.

**This document contains no game content.** No movements, no nations, no scenarios — only real
political traditions, parties, regimes and thinkers. Some of the names below are already in use
elsewhere; they are here because they are real, not because they are ours.

---

## The three axes

| Axis | Low | Middle | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Economy** | Collective | Middle | Neo-liberal |
| **Morals** | Conservative | Middle | Progressive |
| **Government power** | Authoritarian | Middle | Libertarian |

**Economy** — who allocates. Collective means the community or the state owns and directs; neo-liberal
means markets and private ownership decide.

**Morals** — what the good life looks like. Conservative means inherited custom, faith and family are
the standard; progressive means the standard is arrived at and revised.

**Government power** — how much coercion the state may use on people, *independent of what it is
using it for*. This is the axis most spectrums leave out, and the one that separates two groups who
want the same world and disagree completely about whether you may compel it.

Three positions on each of three axes gives **twenty-seven**. They are listed below in three layers
of nine, one layer per economic position.

# Layer one — a collective economy

## 1 · National Collectivism
**Collective / Conservative / Authoritarian**

The state runs the economy on behalf of a traditional people, and it does not ask permission. Welfare
and nationalisation from the left; church, family and nation from the right; no meaningful check on
either.

- **Peronism** (Argentina) — state-directed industry, Catholic traditionalism, personalist rule
- **Ba'athism** (Syria, Iraq) — Arab socialism, social conservatism, a single party
- **Falangism and national syndicalism** under Franco
- **Salazar's *Estado Novo*** in its corporatist phase
- **The Islamic Republic of Iran** — a foundation-dominated economy under clerical authority
- **Huey Long's Share Our Wealth** (Louisiana, 1934) — redistribute the fortunes, keep the social
  order, run the state as a machine
- **Strasserism** — the anti-capitalist wing of national socialism

## 2 · Christian Democracy
**Collective / Conservative / Middle**

A large state sector and a generous welfare system, built explicitly on religious social teaching,
operating through ordinary constitutional politics.

- **Democrazia Cristiana** (Italy) — the IRI state holding company alongside Catholic social doctrine
- **Fianna Fáil** under de Valera — protectionist, Catholic, democratic
- **Adenauer's CDU** and the confessional origins of the social market
- **Bismarck's social insurance** — state pensions and sickness cover to bind workers to a
  traditional order
- **Law and Justice** (Poland) — large family transfers, social conservatism, elected

## 3 · Distributism
**Collective / Conservative / Libertarian**

Property held widely rather than by the state or by capital; morals held tightly; power pushed down
to the smallest unit that can hold it. Communal but voluntary.

- **Chesterton and Belloc**, and *The Servile State*
- **Subsidiarity** in Catholic social teaching — *Rerum Novarum*, *Quadragesimo Anno*
- **The Mormon United Order** and the law of consecration
- **Amish, Hutterite and Mennonite** communities
- **The Catholic Worker Movement** — Dorothy Day and Peter Maurin
- **The Grange** and agrarian cooperative populism

## 4 · Bureaucratic Socialism
**Collective / Middle / Authoritarian**

State ownership without a moral crusade attached. The revolution is over, the ministries remain, and
the ideology has become paperwork.

- **The Soviet Union under Brezhnev** — stagnation, ideology as ritual
- **Tito's Yugoslavia** — market socialism, deliberate neutrality on national and moral questions
- **Nasser's Egypt** — Arab socialism run by officers
- **Ne Win's Burmese Way to Socialism**
- **Post-war state-holding sectors** run as ends in themselves

## 5 · Social Democracy
**Collective / Middle / Middle**

Public ownership of the commanding heights, mainstream social attitudes, and ordinary parliamentary
government. The post-war settlement.

- **Attlee's Labour** (1945–51) — coal, rail, steel, the health service
- **Swedish social democracy** under Erlander and Palme
- **Austria's SPÖ** and the social partnership
- **Nehru's Congress** — the Planning Commission inside a working democracy
- **The post-war consensus** in Britain and its equivalents across western Europe

## 6 · Mutualism
**Collective / Middle / Libertarian**

Wealth held in common through voluntary association rather than the state. Cooperatives, not
ministries.

- **Proudhon's mutualism**
- **The Rochdale Pioneers** and the cooperative movement
- **Mondragón** — a federation of worker cooperatives
- **Guild socialism** (G. D. H. Cole)
- **Friendly societies, building societies and credit unions**
- **Syndicalism** in its non-doctrinal forms

## 7 · Vanguard Socialism
**Collective / Progressive / Authoritarian**

The state owns everything, the future justifies everything, and a disciplined minority decides what
the future is.

- **Marxism–Leninism** — Lenin's *What Is to Be Done?* and the party as vanguard
- **Cuba under Castro**
- **Maoism** and the Cultural Revolution
- **The Jacobins** and the Committee of Public Safety
- **Bolivarianism** under Chávez and Maduro
- **The Sandinistas** in their first decade

## 8 · Democratic Socialism
**Collective / Progressive / Middle**

Common ownership and social liberalisation, pursued by winning elections and accepting the result
when you lose them.

- **Allende's Popular Unity** in Chile — elected, socialist, constitutional
- **The Front Populaire** (France, 1936) and Mitterrand's 1981 programme
- **Labour under Corbyn**; **the Democratic Socialists of America**
- **Syriza** in Greece
- **The Independent Labour Party** and the British ethical-socialist tradition

## 9 · Libertarian Socialism
**Collective / Progressive / Libertarian**

Held in common, decided face to face, and nobody above the assembly. The stateless left.

- **Kropotkin's anarcho-communism**
- **The CNT-FAI in Catalonia**, 1936–37
- **Murray Bookchin's social ecology and communalism**
- **Democratic confederalism in Rojava**, built explicitly on Bookchin
- **The Zapatistas** and the autonomous municipalities of Chiapas
- **The Diggers** and the commune movement

# Layer two — a mixed economy

## 10 · Statist Conservatism
**Middle / Conservative / Authoritarian**

Markets where they work and the state where they don't, in service of an inherited social order, with
elections that do not really threaten anyone.

- **Singapore's People's Action Party** — state-linked capital, social conservatism, managed politics
- **Putin's United Russia** — oligarchic capitalism and Orthodox traditionalism
- **Park Chung-hee's South Korea** — chaebol developmentalism under military rule
- **Erdoğan's AKP**
- **Salazar's Portugal** in its later technocratic phase

## 11 · One Nation Conservatism
**Middle / Conservative / Middle**

The mainstream centre-right of the democratic world: a mixed economy taken as settled, traditional
instincts, constitutional restraint.

- **Macmillan's Conservatives** and the One Nation tradition
- **Gaullism** in France
- **The CDU/CSU** in Germany
- **Japan's Liberal Democratic Party**
- **Eisenhower Republicans**

## 12 · Localist Conservatism
**Middle / Conservative / Libertarian**

A mixed economy, traditional life, and power kept at the parish, the county or the canton. Not
anti-government — anti-*distant* government.

- **Swiss cantonalism** and the *Landsgemeinde*
- **The Anti-Federalists** and Jeffersonian agrarian republicanism
- **Home rule and subsidiarity movements**
- **Front Porch Republic** and the localist-conservative revival
- **The Benedict Option** and withdrawal-into-community arguments

## 13 · Bureaucratic Authoritarianism
**Middle / Middle / Authoritarian**

Rule for the sake of order, with no economic doctrine and no moral programme. The technocrats and the
officers agree only that somebody competent should be in charge.

- **The Brazilian military government**, 1964–85 — the case Guillermo O'Donnell named the type after
- **The Argentine and Chilean juntas** in their administrative aspect
- **Egypt under Sisi**
- **Franco after 1959**, depoliticised and developmentalist
- **Caudillismo** and personalist military rule generally

## 14 · The Centre
**Middle / Middle / Middle**

A mixed economy, moderate social attitudes, ordinary constitutional democracy. The position of the
median voter in almost every functioning democracy, and the one most often mistaken for having no
politics at all.

- **The post-war Western consensus**
- **Erhard's social market economy**
- **The Third Way** — Clinton's New Democrats, Blair's New Labour
- **Macron's En Marche** and continental radical centrism
- **Grand coalitions** wherever they form

## 15 · Federalism
**Middle / Middle / Libertarian**

Not a programme so much as a rule about where decisions are taken: as close to the people affected as
the problem allows.

- **The Swiss Confederation** as a constitutional system
- **Subsidiarity** as a principle of European treaty law
- **Tocqueville's associational America** and civic republicanism
- **Communitarianism** (Amitai Etzioni)
- **The Anti-Federalist case** in its civic rather than agrarian form

## 16 · Authoritarian Modernism
**Middle / Progressive / Authoritarian**

Progress imposed from above on a population that did not ask for it, by a state that considers the
matter settled.

- **Kemalist Turkey** — the alphabet reform, the hat law, forced secularisation
- **The Shah's White Revolution** — land reform and women's suffrage alongside SAVAK
- **Meiji Japan** and compressed modernisation by decree
- **Progressive-era eugenics** and compulsory sterilisation, upheld in *Buck v. Bell*
- **High modernist state planning** — the cities built for a citizen who did not exist yet

## 17 · New Deal Liberalism
**Middle / Progressive / Middle**

A regulated mixed economy and a widening circle of rights, delivered through ordinary majorities.

- **The New Deal coalition** and Roosevelt
- **The Great Society** under Johnson
- **Canada's Liberals** under either Trudeau
- **The SPD** in Germany and the PvdA in the Netherlands
- **Labour under Wilson**

## 18 · Participatory Democracy
**Middle / Progressive / Libertarian**

Progressive ends pursued by dissolving hierarchies rather than capturing them. Suspicious of the
state even while asking it for things.

- **The Port Huron Statement** and Students for a Democratic Society
- **The counterculture and the commune movement**
- **Die Grünen** in their founding anti-statist phase
- **E. F. Schumacher's *Small Is Beautiful***
- **Ivan Illich** — *Deschooling Society*, conviviality, institutional critique

# Layer three — a neo-liberal economy

## 19 · National Conservatism
**Neo-liberal / Conservative / Authoritarian**

Free markets and traditional morals, with a state strong enough to protect the second from the
consequences of the first.

- **Pinochet's Chile** — the Chicago Boys, Catholic traditionalism and a junta in one government
- **Orbán's Hungary** and "illiberal democracy"
- **Integralism** (Adrian Vermeule)
- **Christian Reconstructionism** (R. J. Rushdoony) and dominion theology
- **Colonial Hong Kong** in its socially conservative administration

## 20 · Fusionist Conservatism
**Neo-liberal / Conservative / Middle**

The deliberate welding of free-market economics to social traditionalism, contested at elections. The
dominant right-wing formula of the late twentieth century.

- **Frank Meyer's fusionism** at *National Review*; **Buckley**
- **Reagan** and **Thatcher**
- **The Heritage Foundation** and the think-tank right
- **Howard's Liberal Party** in Australia
- **The Republican Party**, roughly 1980 to 2010

## 21 · Paleolibertarianism
**Neo-liberal / Conservative / Libertarian**

Free markets, inherited morals, and as little state as can be managed — ideally none. Culturally
traditional and radically anti-government at the same time.

- **Murray Rothbard** and **Lew Rockwell**; the Mises Institute
- **Anarcho-capitalism** — Rothbard's *For a New Liberty*, Hoppe's *Democracy: The God That Failed*
- **Robert Taft's Old Right** — anti-New Deal and anti-interventionist
- **Ron Paul**
- **The Sagebrush Rebellion** and the county-supremacy movement
- **The Free State Project**

## 22 · Market Authoritarianism
**Neo-liberal / Middle / Authoritarian**

Maximum economic freedom, minimum political freedom, and no opinion whatsoever about how you live.

- **Colonial Hong Kong** — laissez-faire administration without representation
- **The Gulf states** — Dubai and the UAE: open markets, absolute monarchy
- **Chile's Chicago Boys** in their purely technical aspect
- **Structural adjustment** imposed on states without democratic mandate
- **Special economic zones** governed outside ordinary politics

## 23 · Liberal Centrism
**Neo-liberal / Middle / Middle**

Markets, moderation and institutions. The governing common sense of the late twentieth century,
argued for on grounds of efficiency rather than morality.

- **The Washington Consensus**
- **The Mont Pelerin Society** as it filtered into governing practice
- **Germany's FDP**; the Orange Book wing of the Liberal Democrats
- **The editorial position of *The Economist***

## 24 · Minarchism
**Neo-liberal / Middle / Libertarian**

The state reduced to courts, contracts and defence, and nothing said about what people should do with
the resulting freedom.

- **Nozick's *Anarchy, State, and Utopia***
- **Milton Friedman** and *Capitalism and Freedom*
- **The Libertarian Party** in the United States
- **Hayek's constitutional writing** — *The Constitution of Liberty*
- **Seasteading** and charter-city proposals

## 25 · Technocracy
**Neo-liberal / Progressive / Authoritarian**

Markets are efficient, progress is measurable, expertise is real — and voting mostly gets in the way
of all three.

- **Technocracy Incorporated** (Howard Scott, 1933), a genuine American mass movement
- **Saint-Simon** — the original argument for rule by industrialists and engineers
- **James Burnham's *The Managerial Revolution***
- **Robert Moses**, who rebuilt New York and never won an election
- **Neo-cameralism** (Curtis Yarvin) and **the network state** (Balaji Srinivasan)
- **The Progressive-era expert commission**

## 26 · Social Liberalism
**Neo-liberal / Progressive / Middle**

Open markets, open societies, and ordinary democratic politics. Cosmopolitan by instinct.

- **D66** in the Netherlands
- **Macron** on economics
- **The New Democrats** and the Democratic Leadership Council
- **Canada's Liberals** on trade and immigration
- **The Davos consensus** and its critics' name for it

## 27 · Classical Liberalism
**Neo-liberal / Progressive / Libertarian**

Let people trade, let people be, and keep the state small in *both* directions. Free markets and
social permissiveness as one argument rather than two.

- **John Stuart Mill** — *On Liberty* and the harm principle
- **Cato** and *Reason* — free trade, drug legalisation, open borders, marriage equality, one position
- **John Perry Barlow's *Declaration of the Independence of Cyberspace***; the EFF
- **The cypherpunks** and the early internet
- **Bleeding-heart libertarianism**

## What the shape gives you

The twenty-seven are a cube, three positions on a side, and that geometry carries real consequences
that nobody has to write down as a rule.

| Where a position sits | How many it directly borders |
|---|---|
| A corner — extreme on all three axes | **3** |
| An edge — extreme on two | **4** |
| The middle of a face — extreme on one | **5** |
| The dead centre (**14 · The Centre**) | **6** |

**Extremism is structurally isolating.** A position at a corner has three possible partners in the
whole spectrum; the centre has six. That is not a judgement about extremists — it is arithmetic about
a cube, and it means coalition-building and ideological purity are genuinely opposed without anyone
having to assert that they are.

The eight corners are **1, 3, 7, 9, 19, 21, 25 and 27**. The one position bordering everything is
**14**.
