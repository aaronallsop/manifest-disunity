## Questions inherited from the story of the break-up (6 September)

1. **Wars that happened before turn 0.** Texas fought itself; Houston invaded Louisiana and kept its
   western parishes; the federal army marched into West Virginia and made an enemy of Appalachia.
   How does a war that already happened show on the opening board — weariness, grudges, armies on
   borders, ground already taken? *Built: war weariness rises only with fighting in play; the
   relations vocabulary (warred, annexed, broke) exists and can be back-dated.*

2. **Third parties who fight without declaring.** Louisiana's neighbours "went in to support them"
   — militias and volunteers pushing an invader back. Is there intervention short of war? *Built:
   nothing. A coalition puts border armies in the way, but nobody fights for somebody else.*

3. **An army that is somewhere else.** The federal army opens "stretched thin across the world" and
   guarding the capital. Does force start deployed, depleted, or absent? *Built: force is derived
   from population and wealth every turn; nothing is ever away.*

4. **Inherited assets.** The Gulf nation took the Florida panhandle "because they got access to the
   huge navy base" at Pensacola; the navy was cut in two by the shut canal. Do bases, fleets and
   arsenals exist as things to hold and lose? *Built: no assets; force has no location.*

5. **Terrain.** "The rocky terrain of the Tetons and Rocky Mountains made it hard to invade." Does
   the ground shape a war? *Built: no terrain anywhere in the model.*

6. **Conquering stateless ground.** New Mexico's remainder is "ripe for conquering" by El Paso,
   Oklahoma, the Navajo Nation or the Front Range Republic. What does taking tier-3 ground cost,
   whom does it anger, and who defends it? *Depends on politics' answer to what tier 3 is.*

7. **What defends a small nation?** Observed in play: Maine, Vermont and New Hampshire were eaten
   early. The anti-snowball devices stop a big conqueror; nothing stops a middling neighbour eating
   a three-Area state, because a small predator frightens nobody. *Built: coalition threat is size
   share × (1 − influence), so it never fires for this case.* Aaron's answer for New England was to
   consolidate; the general question stands.

8. **Annexations authored at open.** Chicago has already taken north-west Indiana; the Front Range
   is pushing into New Mexico; Houston holds western Louisiana. Do opening conquests carry weariness
   and grudges, or are they settled facts?

9. **Unite lives here** (per the plan). The story's Texas is five nations each trying to reunify
   Texas under itself — by force or by persuasion. Is Unite the peaceful half of that, and does a
   failed union between two Texases shatter the proposer as built?
