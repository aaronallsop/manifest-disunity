## Questions inherited from the story of the break-up (6 September)

1. **The break-up as a chain of events.** Texas seceded → the five fell out → oil stopped → the US
   would not recognise the successors, so gas could not leave → shortages everywhere → trust in
   Washington collapsed → protests at the capital → martial law → local powers stepped up. Is the
   backstory told as a sequence of dated events in the opening newspaper — and could the same chain
   run *forward* in play, for some other nation, from some other spark? *Built: twelve authored
   crises, each triggered by a nation's own condition — symptoms with choices attached, not shocks
   from outside; the opening edition prints three sentences.*

2. **Mass protests at the capital.** An event with a choice — concede, crush, wait — that already has
   cousins in the crisis deck (*the veterans march*, *clear the square*).

3. **A world shock.** The oil stoppage hit every nation at once. The first exogenous event the game
   would need, and the one the economy round will have to price. *(Shared with economy, E85.)*

4. **A neighbour comes apart.** Exists as a crisis — open the border or close it. The story wants
   the people who arrive to carry their politics with them. *(Shared with secession, S33.)*

5. **Events as the backstory's voice** (F12, F14). The canal, the bicentenary, the year the Union
   dissolved — the game has nowhere yet to say any of it.
