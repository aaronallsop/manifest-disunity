# Old ideas

**Things taken out of a design, kept because they are not bad — just not for now.**

Nothing here is deleted and nothing here is dead. An entry arrives when Aaron strikes something from a
working document; it leaves when it is picked up again, either by the round that turns out to own it
or by a later pass that wants it back. If an idea is *deferred rather than removed*, it belongs in
`docs/FUTURE-IDEAS.md` instead; this file is for things that were in a design and came out of it.

Each entry says what it was, why it was struck, and where it went.

---

## From round 1 — the Movement Register, 7 September 2026

Aaron marked up all 32 movements in the register and struck six. **Four of them were struck because
they are not movements at all — they are political leanings**, which is a different system; two were
struck as movements outright.

### Struck because they turned out to be politics, not movements

These four carried the *ideological* adjective and no verb: they have no country at the end of them,
so under ruling 39 they have nothing to do. Aaron's own reading, in his words, is that they "became
political things that I think we should move to work on later."

**They are the seed of a political-alignment question that came up three separate times in the same
markup**, and it should be settled in the politics round rather than here.

| Struck | What it was | Where it should be picked up |
|---|---|---|
| **Libertarians** | The Great Plains — Oklahoma, Kansas, Nebraska, the Dakotas. 94 Areas, core 30. *Minimal government; free markets* | **A seventh political alignment.** Aaron raised Libertarianism independently on the Sagebrush Rebellion and the Central States Union: "we might need to add another political alignment with Libertarianism", and "including libertarian later would help solve some of this" |
| **Anarcho-Capitalist** | The Montana interior. 38 Areas, core 4. *Abolish the state; free markets* | The far end of the same alignment. Note the resonance with the stateless-ground design: this is where tier 3 already sits |
| **Eastern Progressives** | East Coast metros over 500,000 plus the Hudson valley. 84 Areas, core 30. *Social democracy; civil liberties* | A leaning, sitting close to the existing Democratic Socialist position |
| **Techno-Autocrat** | Silicon Valley, San Francisco, Austin, Seattle, Cambridge, Salt Lake. 11 Areas, core 5. *Technocracy; network states* | A leaning with no home on the current two axes at all — which may be an argument for a third axis, or for leaving it out |

**Two of the six ideological movements survived** and were given verbs instead, so the category was
not struck wholesale: **Blue-Collar Populist** became *Unify / economic* and **Christian Nationalism**
became *Unify / ideological*.

### Struck as movements

| Struck | What it was | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Delmarva Republic** | The Delmarva peninsula, southern Maryland and the South Jersey shore. 22 Areas, core 7, cap 0.35. *Statehood; watermen's rights* | Struck without a note. Worth knowing before it is reinstated: under ruling 1 this ground is inside Philadelphia's and Washington's territory, so the movement would have grown inside two city-states rather than inside a state |
| **Fifty-First State** | The Colorado plains east of Denver. 41 Areas, core 3, cap 0.40. *Statehood; an end to Denver's rules* | Struck without a note. It was the internal opposition to the Front Range Republic — which Aaron moved from *Separate* to *Expand* in the same pass, so the nation it was going to fight is now looking outward instead |

**If either is wanted back**, both are real politics — the Delmarva peninsula has petitioned for its
own statehood more than once, and eleven Colorado plains counties actually voted on secession in
November 2013 — and both homelands are still baked in `data/parties.json`.
