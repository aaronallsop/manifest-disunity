## Questions inherited from the story of the break-up (6 September)

1. **Alliances.** Washington and Oregon; the United States of New England and Rochester-and-Buffalo.
   What is an alliance — mutual defence, shared corridors, a say in each other's wars, all of it?
   *Built: a non-aggression pact, with a cooldown and a minimum standing; a broken one is remembered
   as `reneged`. No alliance.*

2. **Vassals.** Oklahoma "became essentially a vassal state to Dallas, which was great for Dallas
   because that gave them a buffer." What does a vassal owe, and what does it get? Can vassalage be
   demanded — Houston tried it on Louisiana, was refused, and invaded? *Built: nothing.*

3. **Blocs.** The Farmers Union: eight states supporting each other's farming and protecting their
   corridors to Canada and down the Mississippi. A standing, multilateral, economic agreement — and
   Chicago *left* it. What does membership give, what does leaving cost, and can a bloc admit a
   member or expel one? *Built: coalitions form only against a threat and dissolve when it passes;
   every deal is bilateral.*

4. **Recognition as policy.** The United States "refused to treat Texas as a sovereign nation" and
   would not deal with the mountain states, which is why gas could not leave Texas and why all
   trade funnelled through Arizona. Does the federal remnant open recognising nobody, and does the
   built trade block then cut it off from everyone at once? *Built: the recognition matrix and the
   bilateral trade block — exactly the mechanism the story describes.*

5. **The three tiers of standing.** Recognised / secessionist / stateless. Are the top two simply
   *recognised* and *unrecognised by the parent*, as built? What standing does tier-3 ground have —
   Aaron says it "makes its own deals", so somebody signs for it; who? *Depends on politics'
   definition (round 3).*

6. **Agreements signed before the game starts.** The New England–Rochester alliance and its
   twenty-year free-trade deal; the Farmers Union; Washington–Oregon. *Built: the opening scenario
   authors nations, grudges and sentiment — not deals, corridors or pacts. The same pattern,
   extended.*

7. **Ultimatums.** Houston made Louisiana an offer "hoping that a show of force would make them
   change their mind." A demand backed by a threat, refused. *Built: negotiation exists for deals and
   corridors, not for territory or submission.*

8. **Intervention.** Louisiana's neighbours came to its defence uninvited. Mutual defence without a
   treaty. *(Shared with conquest, question 2 there.)*

9. **Funding somebody else's movement.** *(Aaron, 7 September — secession ruling 21, S71.)* A nation
   pays a share of its treasury every quarter to grow a movement inside a neighbour, and everyone can
   see it. Does that create a memory, and does it draw a coalition? *Built: the relations vocabulary
   already carries `betrayed` — "they admitted our rebels are a country" — and paying them is worse
   than admitting them; coalitions form on threat, which this would have to feed.*

10. **The petition, and everything shaped like it — this round owns the mechanism.** *(Aaron,
    7 September, secession rulings 47–50.)* Round 1 settled what a petition *is*: a movement that has
    grown strong enough approaches the nation or nations that could carry it, on the model of Texas
    asking to join the United States and being refused for years for reasons that had nothing to do
    with Texas. It is a **standing offer**, not a one-shot; the answer can be *not yet*; **several
    nations can be petitioned and compete for it**, decided by nerve rather than arithmetic, because
    accepting costs the accepting nation whatever it was before — El Paso gives up its claim to
    reunify Texas, Oregon stops being Oregon. **Refusing costs nothing immediately and everything
    eventually**: the movement keeps growing, a rival may take it, and one refused often enough stops
    asking and falls back to its own verb, becoming a country on your border that remembers.

    **What this round owns:** how an offer is made, held open, bid for and withdrawn; whether a nation
    can *solicit* a petition rather than wait to be asked (the other half of sponsorship, secession
    ruling 21); whether a refused movement writes a memory, and which one — `betrayed` exists for a
    related injury. *Built: negotiation exists for trade deals and corridors, with a term, a fixed
    price and an expiry. A petition is the same shape pointed at territory and allegiance, which is
    the reuse worth checking first.*

11. **Recognise lives here**, as built: costs no money, takes no action, priced with the parent.
